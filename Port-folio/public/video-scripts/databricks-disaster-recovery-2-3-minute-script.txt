This project was about making a critical Databricks platform recoverable without turning disaster recovery into a manual runbook. I designed and implemented a primary-to-secondary workspace architecture that covered replication, outage detection, failover, reverse synchronization, and the return to normal operations.

The original business risk was straightforward: if the primary workspace became unavailable, analytics workloads and downstream reporting could stop. A useful recovery design therefore needed more than a second workspace. It had to keep recoverable copies current, detect a real outage, avoid conflicting writes during failover, and move reports to whichever environment was active.

During normal operation, workloads run in the primary workspace. I used Delta Sharing to expose primary catalog data securely and read-only to the secondary workspace. Scheduled DeepClone workflows then create independent copies in the secondary catalogs every six hours. Alongside that data path, I built a CLI-based health monitor to check the availability of the primary workspace, its important clusters, and workflows.

When the monitor detects that the primary environment is unavailable, the orchestration changes state. Replication and DeepClone activity are paused to protect consistency, while the required secondary workflows are resumed. Fabric shortcuts are then repointed through APIs so reports and dashboards follow the secondary environment instead of continuing to depend on the unavailable primary workspace.

Recovery is equally important. When the monitor confirms that the primary workspace is healthy again, secondary processing is paused to prevent conflicts. Reverse DeepClone jobs synchronize changes made during failover back into the primary catalogs. Only after those synchronization steps complete do primary workflows resume, Delta Sharing restart, and the regular secondary-clone schedule return. Fabric shortcuts are also switched back to the primary storage location.

My contribution was the end-to-end DR lifecycle: the replication pattern, independent secondary copies, health-monitoring logic, workflow-state orchestration, reporting failover, and controlled recovery. The portfolio records 99.9 percent availability, an RPO below one hour, a 30-minute RTO, and zero verified data loss. The important outcome is that recovery is a coordinated process with explicit safeguards, rather than an improvised response after an outage.
