Putting data in one lakehouse does not automatically make it governed. Teams still need clear answers to basic questions: Who owns this data product? Who is allowed to use it? Which environment does it belong to? And where did the data come from? This project addressed those questions with Unity Catalog. The problem was a permission model centered on individual workspaces, combined with inconsistent ownership. That made it difficult for engineering and analytics teams to understand access, trace lineage, and identify which team was accountable for a data product. I designed a catalog-first operating model for the Databricks lakehouse. Registered Delta assets were organized through catalogs and schemas aligned to data domains and environments. I standardized naming, ownership, grants, and environment boundaries so the structure communicated how each product should be managed. For access, I used a least-privilege approach based on groups rather than one-off permissions for individuals. That makes the access model easier to reason about and supports controlled consumption of production data products. Unity Catalog served as the shared governance layer, carrying permissions, ownership, descriptions, metadata, and lineage instead of leaving each workspace or consumer to recreate them. Lineage was another important part of the design. Engineering and analytics teams could discover the origin and downstream path of a data product through the centralized catalog. This connected technical movement with accountable ownership and made the data easier to inspect before use. My role was Data Engineer within a cross-functional data platform team. My contribution was not only configuring a technology; it was defining an operating model around it: how data products are named, where they live, who owns them, how access is granted, and how production boundaries are controlled. The production outcome is a governed lakehouse with Unity Catalog as the centralized control plane and end-to-end data-product lineage. The catalog does not record the number of users, assets, workspaces, permission groups, or access requests, so I do not attach an unverified scale or efficiency percentage. The verified value is structural: consistent organization, least-privilege group access, visible accountability, and auditable paths from registered Delta data to approved data-team consumption.
